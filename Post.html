Mountains

Three passes, one bad map, and the best soup of the trip

By Emaan March 12, 2026 6 min read

The plan was three days. It became four, mostly because of a map that was drawn, as far as we can tell, from memory rather than measurement. That extra day turned out to be the best one.

We started from the lower village a little after sunrise, while the tea houses were still setting out their morning fires. The first pass is the easiest of the three — a long, steady climb through pine that thins out slowly enough that you barely notice the tree line until it's behind you.

Where the map gave up

Somewhere past the second pass, the trail marked on our map simply stopped existing. What we found instead was a herder's path, considerably steeper, that a local family pointed us toward with a level of confidence that turned out to be entirely justified.

"You won't get lost," the herder told us. "There's only one way down that doesn't end in a cliff."

He was right, technically. It was also, by a wide margin, the hardest two hours of the trip.

The soup

We reached the last village well after dark, cold enough that conversation had mostly stopped. The guesthouse had one dish left on the stove — a thick, unlabelled soup that we still haven't been able to identify — and it remains, unreasonably, one of the best things either of us has eaten anywhere.

If you're planning this route yourself: budget four days, not three, carry more layers than you think you need, and don't trust the tree-line contour lines on the older maps.
